Bank account lien or freeze after a cybercrime complaint (1930): what to do

If your bank says a lien or debit freeze is linked to a cybercrime complaint, first find out exactly what is restricted and which authority asked the bank to act. A restriction is not, by itself, proof of wrongdoing. Keep your communication factual and use verified bank or police contact channels.

Important: There is no single guaranteed “unfreeze” process or universal release timeline. The bank may be acting on instructions from a State/UT law-enforcement agency. The steps below are practical information, not legal advice.

1. Ask the bank what kind of hold it is

Contact the bank using its official app, branch, website, or a number printed on your card or statement. Ask in writing whether it is:

  • a lien/hold on a specific amount, or
  • a debit restriction on some or all of the account.

Request the amount, relevant transaction date and UTR/reference, the bank’s complaint number, the issuing authority and State/UT, the investigating officer’s official contact, and the process for sending documents. Ask for the order or reference details if the bank is allowed to share them; some details may be restricted.

2. Gather records before you write

Keep copies of the bank alert, relevant account statement, transaction/UTR details, and records that show the source and purpose of the funds—such as an invoice, sale agreement, salary record, or messages with the sender. Provide identity/KYC documents only when requested through a verified channel. Keep every complaint number, email, delivery proof, and acknowledgement.

3. Send a written request to the bank and issuing authority

Ask the bank’s grievance or nodal officer how to submit a representation and whether it can forward it to the issuing authority. Send a concise, factual request to the investigating officer or cybercrime/police contact named by the bank. Attach only relevant evidence. Ask for review and release of the hold, or release of any unaffected funds/restriction to the disputed amount if legally permissible. The bank may not be able to remove a police-directed hold on its own.

4. If you are the person reporting a financial cyber-fraud

Call India’s financial cyber-fraud helpline 1930 promptly. If you receive an acknowledgement number, the I4C FAQ says to complete the formal complaint at the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal within 24 hours using that number. Save the acknowledgement and use the portal to track the complaint. I4C says complaints are handled by the relevant State/UT law-enforcement agency.

A 1930 report is a way to report financial cyber-fraud; it does not guarantee that an existing bank hold will be lifted. If your account is restricted, also follow up with the bank and the issuing authority identified in the bank’s notice.

5. Escalate through the appropriate channel

  • For a bank communication or service issue, use the bank’s official grievance process. RBI publishes complaint information and bank grievance links. The RBI Complaint Management System handles eligible complaints against regulated entities; it is not a substitute for asking law enforcement or a court to review a police-directed hold.
  • If the investigating authority does not respond, you dispute the transaction link, the whole account is blocked, or the restriction causes urgent hardship, consult an Indian lawyer. A lawyer can advise whether to approach the appropriate court for review or partial release. The correct procedure depends on the case, authority, and any court directions.

Protect yourself from “unfreeze” scams

Do not pay an agent, caller, or social-media account promising guaranteed release. Never share OTPs, PINs, passwords, or remote-access codes. Do not move funds to evade a restriction, make false statements, or delete records. Verify contacts using the bank’s official website/app or a written notice, not an unsolicited caller or search advertisement.
